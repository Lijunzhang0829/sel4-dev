## Structural signature
The proof contains an unconstrained automation tactic — blast, fastforce, auto, force, or crush — that performs exhaustive hypothesis search over the full goal/assumption context. Such tactics can silently consume a premise that is textually absent from the proof body: they receive all current hypotheses and close goals by exhaustive search, so removing one hypothesis (even one never mentioned by name) may leave a subgoal that the tactic can no longer close. This is precisely the failure mode observed in the LOSSES: the dropped head is absent from the proof text (satisfying signal #1's unused-premise criterion), yet the automation was silently depending on it to discharge one or more subgoals produced by definition unfolding or earlier steps.

## Why it's novel (not an existing signal)
Signal #1 (unused-premise) already detects that the dropped head is absent from the proof text — but it does not distinguish WHY the proof can or cannot succeed without it. Signals #2–#5 look at conjunct consumption, frame premises, op class, and implication scope. Signal #6 looks at whether the head feeds a specific named invoked rule's precondition. The new signal is orthogonal to all six: it looks at the PROOF STRATEGY (not the premise, not named rules, not op class) and says that unconstrained automation makes any textually-absent premise unsafe to drop, because the tactic's success depended on the full assumption context. It fires purely on the presence of specific tactic tokens in the proof field.

## Proposed predicate (for calibration; review before promoting)
```python
def proposed_signal(feat):
    import re
    proof = feat.get('proof', '') or ''
    # Unconstrained automation tactics consume all available hypotheses.
    # A premise absent from proof text can still be load-bearing if any such
    # tactic is the discharging step. Fires conservatively only on these tokens.
    return bool(re.search(r'\b(blast|fastforce|auto|force|crush)\b', proof))
```
